Photosynthesis

The fundamental biological process that converts light energy into chemical energy, fueling life on Earth through the synthesis of organic compounds.

Mechanism

Photosynthesis occurs primarily within the leaves of plants, specifically inside specialized organelles called chloroplasts. These structures house chlorophyll, the pigment responsible for capturing solar radiation. The intricate dance of electrons and protons within the thylakoid membranes represents one of nature's most efficient energy conversion systems.

The process of photosynthesis is divided into two main stages: the light-dependent reactions and the light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle). The former takes place in the thylakoid membranes and uses light energy to make ATP and NADPH. The latter takes place in the stroma and uses those products to capture and reduce carbon dioxide.

Without this critical mechanism, the atmospheric oxygen levels would plummet, and the foundational layers of the global food web would collapse. It is the bridge between the celestial energy of our sun and the biological reality of our planet.
