Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is a process used by plants and other organisms to convert light energy into chemical energy that, through cellular respiration, can later be released to fuel the organism's activities. This chemical energy is stored in carbohydrate molecules, such as sugars and starches, which are synthesized from carbon dioxide and water.

Mechanism

The process of photosynthesis is divided into two main stages: the light-dependent reactions and the light-independent reactions (also known as the Calvin cycle). In the light-dependent reactions, chlorophyll absorbs energy from sunlight and then converts it into chemical energy with the use of water.

The light-dependent reactions release oxygen as a byproduct as water is broken apart. The light-independent reactions use the energy produced by the light-dependent reactions to form carbohydrates from carbon dioxide

. In most plants, photosynthesis occurs in the leaves, where the chloroplasts are concentrated.

In simple terms, light-dependent reactions are the "charging" phase. They happen in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts:

  1. Sunlight Capture: Chlorophyll traps light energy.
  2. Water Splitting: This energy splits water molecules, releasing Oxygen (what we breathe!).
  3. Energy Storage: The energy is stored in molecules called ATP and NADPH, which act like full batteries for the next stage.
