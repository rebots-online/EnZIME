01 · The Problem

Knowledge is offline-shaped.
The tools aren't.

ZIM files already hold human knowledge in a single sideloadable archive. Every reader built on top of them throws away three things that matter.

02 · The Product — Chat-with-ZIM

Ask the archive. It answers — on-device.

Point a question at the archive and an on-device model answers against the actual archive content, not just its own training data. In airplane mode. By voice if you want. Try it — pick a question below, or type your own.

Every device gets a working AI

A first-launch probe benchmarks the device and picks the largest model it can sustain — then falls down a five-rung ladder if needed. The bottom rung ships inside the binary, so the app has a working AI even on a phone that has never touched a network.

  • Voice questions ride the model's native audio encoder — speak to the archive, no separate STT engine.
  • Dual-tier weights: a ~500 MB universal floor, a ~3 GB full-multimodal upgrade for devices that pass a real capability check — capability, not a marketing tier.
  • Cloud is a terminal rung behind an explicit per-conversation opt-in. The app never silently routes your reading to someone else's server.
03 · The Product — Dynamic Download ’Daption

100 GB is an all-or-nothing* everywhere else.
We made it a slider.

Full English Wikipedia is ~100 GB. Your phone has 12 GB free. Every other reader forces the impossible download or nothing at all — so the answer is usually nothing. EnZIME asks one human question — how much can you spare? — picks a sane default from the storage actually free this session, and derives the depth, breadth, and media tradeoff for you. As storage wanes or replenishes, the plan re-derives. The alternative isn't a worse experience; it's no access at all. Move the sliders.

    Why this is a blocker, not a convenience

    The store itself is the wall — and it falls hardest on the people who need the archive most.

    An app on Google Play cannot just ship a 100 GB archive. Play caps what an app delivers — roughly a 200 MB base download and about a 4 GB ceiling even with Play Asset Delivery — and apps that side-load massive downloads outside the store's own mechanisms risk policy rejection. So the shelf options collapse to two: a small curated bundle the store permits, or "go fetch the whole 100 GB yourself" — which no phone can hold.

    For a casual reader that's an annoyance. For a prepper, a field medic, a teacher in a low-bandwidth region — the people for whom offline knowledge is the entire point — it means the vital archive is effectively unreachable on the device they actually carry. The platform's policy, not the technology, is the blocker.

    EnZIME removes the wall from both sides: the planner makes the archive fit the device, and the suite also ships through sideload APK and direct desktop channels — so a store's size policy can never stand between a user and the knowledge they're carrying for a reason.

    Highlights, notes, voice, ink — and a notebook that's always beside you

    • The notebook is co-visible, not a separate tab. You never memorize a passage and context-switch away to record it — reader and notebook share the screen, so synthesis happens in place.
    • Synthesize across sessions. Today's highlight files itself next to notes from earlier articles and earlier days; the notebook is where a week of reading becomes one connected understanding.
    • Rich-text highlights & bookmarks on any span, plus voice notes (spoken margin comments) and drawing (ink pinned to the passage) — all anchored to the same range, surviving re-render and font changes.
    • The anti-loss promise: if anchoring ever genuinely fails, the annotation is kept with a "needs reattach" badge — never silently dropped.
    05 · Sharing & the Knowledge Mesh

    Your highlights travel as a file.

    Annotations live as a portable sidecar keyed to the archive — never embedded in it. That one decision makes them exportable, shareable, and composable, with no server in the loop and no lock-in.
